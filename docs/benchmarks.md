# Benchmarks

## Honest about what is measured here

Two separate things, deliberately kept apart:

1. **Latency** — wall-clock for `SCRFD` detect and `ArcFace` embed on this machine,
   with the available backend. Numbers from one machine do not generalise, so these
   are reported with the hardware identified, not as absolutes.
2. **Cosine similarity distributions** — same-identity vs different-identity. The
   *gap* between those two is what a threshold must fit into, and it is the actual
   input to picking one. The numbers below come from `tests/fixtures/{lena,biden}.ppm`,
   a 2-face sample, so they are illustrative not statistical.

For absolute detection accuracy on a public benchmark, see `docs/bench-results.md`
generated by `cargo run --release --bin bench-accuracy`. The WIDER FACE numbers quoted
in `README.md` come from the upstream papers and are cited as such.

## Measured numbers — `cargo run --release --bin bench-accuracy`

Hardware: Apple M4 Pro, 11 cores. Backend: `ort` (ONNX Runtime 1.29) on CPU, single
thread per session. Fixture: `tests/fixtures/lena.ppm` 512×512.

```
backend: ort
fixture: lena.ppm 512x512, 1 in frame

-- latency (ms) --
  detect (SCRFD+postprocess): n=30 mean=293.01 p50=291.08 p95=303.71 min=286.61 max=332.29
  embed (ArcFace): n=30 mean=134.61 p50=134.33 p95=141.05 min=130.86 max=145.83

-- cosine similarity --
  same identity: n=20 mean=1.00 p50=1.00 p95=1.00 min=1.00 max=1.00
  different identity: n=20 mean=0.07 p50=0.07 p95=0.07 min=0.07 max=0.07
  margin (mean same - mean diff): 0.9315
  recommended threshold (midpoint worst-same/best-diff): 0.5342
```

### Why the default threshold is 0.36, and why your data may need a different one

The crate default of 0.36 is InsightFace's recommended operating point for `w600k_r50`
at TAR@FAR=1e-4, derived from a large held-out evaluation, **not** from the
same-vs-different mid-point on any specific pair. The two are different objectives:

* **TAR@FAR=1e-4** picks the threshold that achieves 99.99% specificity on a large
  verification test. The actual number depends on the test's demographics.
* **Midpoint** of two distributions is the threshold where a fresh sample is equally
  likely to be classified either way. It is a reasonable **starting** point but not
  what you should ship without your own TAR/FAR measurement.

The benchmark above shows the midpoint on a 2-face sample is 0.53 — *well above* 0.36,
because the worst same-identity score in the sample was 1.0 (no embedding noise here
since we embed the same pixels twice) and the best different-identity score was 0.07.
With more identities the distributions will broaden; on a sample of 2 the distributions
are *under-estimates* of their real spread. Production thresholds for face recognition
are routinely in the 0.5-0.7 range depending on the camera, lighting, and identity
mix. The default here is conservative enough to work as a placeholder; raise it as
your own data justifies.

### How to recalibrate on your own data

```rust
use rsface::embedding::{Gallery, MatchConfig};

let cfg = MatchConfig::default().with_threshold(0.53);   // your measured midpoint
let mut gallery = Gallery::new(cfg);
```

For a more principled value, compute TAR@FAR across a labelled probe/enrol set:

```text
TAR(s)  = fraction of same-identity pairs with cosine >= s
FAR(s)  = fraction of different-identity pairs with cosine >= s
pick s to satisfy  FAR(s) <= 1e-4 (or whatever your application requires)
```

The bench is the seed for a real ROC curve. Add more fixtures under `tests/fixtures/`,
extend `benches/accuracy.rs` to report TAR and at the chosen FAR, and the threshold
falls out rather than being guessed.

## GPU offload

The `ort-backend` feature exposes `Device::{Cuda, TensorRt, CoreMl, DirectMl}`. Setting
`SessionConfig::with_device(Device::Cuda(0))` (and adding `--features ort-cuda`)
routes inference onto the GPU. The benchmark above is CPU-only; on a discrete NVIDIA
GPU we expect ~10-40x speedup on the same model, matching ORT published numbers.

## What is *not

- WIDER FACE AP measured on this repo. We cite upstream figures because the WIDER
  FACE dataset is large and downloading it would dominate the build setup. Add the
  dataset and re-run the bench harness if you need a fresh in-house number.
- IJB-C TAR@FAR on this repo. Same reason: dataset is large and the InsightFace
  paper numbers are authoritative.
